User Persona
Alex Williams is a 35-year-old marketing manager living in Cambridge, MA, who has spent years collecting recipes and now has over 200 of them spread across several cookbooks in CookYourBooks. The Technical Comfort is 4/5. He's a confident, high-comfort tech user the kind of person who lives in Gmail, Notion, and Spotify and he brings those same expectations to recipe software: results should appear as he types, filters should be right there when he needs them, and nothing should make him wait.

Goals:
What he wants most is a search experience that feels as snappy and smart as the tools he already uses every day one that remembers his recent searches so he doesn't have to retype the same queries, surfaces filters like cookbook, tag, prep time, and servings without sending him to a different screen, and shows enough in each result row (a thumbnail, the cookbook name, the time) that he can tell recipes apart at a glance. He wants filters like cookbook, tag, prep time, and servings to be right there on the search screen so he never has to navigate away mid-search. He wants each result to show like a thumbnail, the cookbook name, the prep time that he can pick the right one without opening everything.

Pain Points:
The frustrations he runs into everywhere else are painfully familiar: apps that return every recipe mentioning "lemon" in a step instead of ranking title matches first, filters hidden in settings menus that break his flow mid-search, no history of what he searched last week, and results stripped of context so he can't tell which cookbook something came from. He's usually searching on his phone with the stove already on, so every extra tap or slow load feels like a problem he needs the interface to stay out of his way and get him to the right recipe before anything burns.

Context:
When he opens the app, it's almost always to search. He doesn't browse the library grid and he types "chicken" or "korean food" and expects to see the right recipe in seconds, either because he's standing in front of the fridge figuring out dinner or because someone across the table just asked him for a recipe and he needs to pull it up fast. He needs results to show up as he types and he wants the ranking to be smart enough to put the most relevant results first and he needs the whole experience to be fast enough that nothing burns while he's looking.
